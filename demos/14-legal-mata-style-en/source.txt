In a 2023 sanctions order, a United States federal district court found
that the attorneys of record for the plaintiff had submitted a brief that
relied on six citations to judicial opinions which, on the court's
inspection, did not exist in any legal reporter or docket. The court
determined that the attorneys had generated the citations using a
public AI chat tool and had failed to verify the cited cases against any
authoritative legal database before filing.

The order rejected the attorneys' argument that the failure was the
fault of the AI tool. The court ruled that attorneys owe an independent
duty of candor to the tribunal and may not delegate the verification of
authority to a generative AI system. The court explicitly sanctioned the
two attorneys and their firm, imposing a monetary penalty of five
thousand dollars jointly and severally, ordering the attorneys to
notify each judge falsely named in the fabricated opinions, and
requiring the attorneys to complete an additional course of professional
responsibility training before resuming representation.

The opinion closed by stating that the sanction was not entered because
of the use of an AI tool in itself, but because the attorneys
abandoned the verification step that the rules of professional conduct
require regardless of the drafting medium. The court declined to find
bad-faith intent but found the conduct objectively unreasonable under
Rule 11.
