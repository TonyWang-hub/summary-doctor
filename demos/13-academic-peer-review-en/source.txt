A four-page literature review on peer-review feedback mechanisms reports
the results of a 2024 controlled study with 142 participants drawn from a
single mid-sized research institution. The authors found that targeted
written feedback, delivered alongside a structured rubric, reduced
peer-review turnaround time by 18 percent relative to free-form feedback,
and that the reduction was statistically significant at the 0.01 level
(Smith et al., 2024, Journal of Synthetic Reviews).

The review further reports that this effect was confined to early-career
researchers — defined in the source paper as reviewers within five years
of their first independent appointment. Among senior reviewers in the
same sample, the same combination of targeted feedback and structured
rubric did not produce a statistically significant change in turnaround
time, and the authors explicitly caution against generalizing the 18
percent figure outside the early-career subgroup.

The literature review closes by noting two boundary conditions that the
original controlled study did not test: the effect was measured only in
single-blind review tracks, and only for manuscripts under 8,000 words.
The authors recommend replication before any policy recommendation is
drawn from the 18 percent figure.
