The author proposes a three-stage framework for evaluating retrieval-augmented
generation systems. Stage one is retrieval quality, measured by recall at k and
mean reciprocal rank against a labeled set. Stage two is groundedness, measured
by what fraction of generated sentences can be attributed to a retrieved
passage. Stage three is end-to-end utility, measured by downstream task success.

The author argues that benchmarks reporting only stage-one metrics are
misleading. High retrieval recall is insufficient for groundedness; high
groundedness is insufficient for downstream task success.
